# Sound design for coenx-video

Adapted from Barty-Bart's MIT-licensed sound-effects skill. The original sound pack, source credits and per-asset license notes are retained in third_party/motion-graphics/skills/sound-effects/. Read its LICENSES.md, MUSIC.md and TOP-PICKS.md before using individual files.

## Choose the sound's job

Use a restrained sound pass. Give each cue a reason:

- **Ambience:** establish a place or a quiet tone; keep it low and continuous.
- **UI click/select:** mark an intentional action, not every frame or hover.
- **Confirmation:** reinforce a completed action.
- **Impact / transition:** support a true visual change or reveal.
- **Glitch:** use sparingly and only when the content calls for it.
- **Music:** establish pace and emotional context without competing with narration.
- **Voice:** explain something that would be ambiguous without speech.

Silence is a valid choice. Do not add random bleeps to every interaction.

## Sound library

Source assets live under:
third_party/motion-graphics/skills/sound-effects/sfx/

Use only assets whose source license is explicitly recorded as compatible in the upstream LICENSES.md. The upstream README identifies the included SFX pack as CC0 1.0; still consult the specific attribution/source entry for each sound. Keep the upstream license and credits with any copied asset. Do not assume music tracks are CC0: consult MUSIC.md and use only user-supplied or explicitly cleared music.

Copy selected assets into the video project directory before rendering; the coenx renderer accepts project-relative local paths, not remote URLs. Avoid copying the entire repository or all sounds into every export.

## Standard-video mix

Attach each sound as an AudioClip with a stable id, local source path, kind, startFrame, durationFrames and a sensible volume. Use trimStartSeconds, fadeInFrames and fadeOutFrames when needed. Keep music lower than dialogue. Use no more than the minimum number of simultaneous layers; avoid clipping and let the renderer's limiter catch peaks, not replace a balanced mix.

The renderer mixes the authored audio clips into the MP4. It does not synthesize audio or perform automatic speech/music separation. If the voice track is generated by a provider, include it as a local voiceover asset and keep the provider/model provenance in project notes.

## Interactive audio

Use playAudio actions on clicks, answers, feedback or scene transitions. Start music on the first user gesture when the browser's autoplay policy requires an unlock. Short SFX should remain short; do not replay a success sound every frame or timer tick.

## Verification

Listen to the full mix, including quiet parts and transitions. Inspect the final MP4 for audio presence and duration. Keep dialogue intelligible, SFX below speech, and avoid repeated cues that become irritating during a lesson. For interactive lessons, test audio after at least one user gesture and after a scene change.

Never create a soundtrack from a file whose license or permission is unknown.
