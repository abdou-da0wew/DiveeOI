# Storyboard and visual narrative

Translate the user's goal into one viewer takeaway. Choose the format before writing scenes: standard MP4 for passive viewing, interactive player for decisions or learner input.

Build a scene plan with scene ID, frame duration, purpose, visual metaphor, exact copy, motion, audio cues, evidence source and transition. Open with a clear hook, establish context, show the mechanism or proof, then close with a conclusion or action. A short video still needs a complete beginning, middle and end.

Prefer visual explanations over decorative transitions. Use scale, position, grouping, contrast, sequence and annotation to show relationships. Avoid generic stock metaphors when a concrete diagram or supplied product visual is more truthful.

Match pace to audience and reading load. Allow extra time for equations, charts, unfamiliar terminology, Arabic captions and learner interaction. Never invent product claims, study results, citations, statistics, quotations, or scientific evidence to make a storyboard feel compelling.

Keep narration, on-screen copy and visuals synchronized. On-screen text should reinforce the narration rather than duplicate every sentence. Keep music and sound effects subordinate to spoken explanation and critical feedback.\n
